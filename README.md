<h1>Hello, friend.</h1>

Hello, friend? That feels wrong. Names always do. They give things weight before you know what they are. After all you are not real. You are just a way for me to organize the noise. That matters. Because once you start treating voices like people, you stop questioning where they came from.

That’s how trust works. It slips in quietly. You don’t agree to it, you just get used to it being there. Systems depend on that. Not correctness. Not truth. Familiarity. As long as nothing breaks, no one asks why it works.

But things are always breaking. Just not where people are looking.
Most coders think debugging is about fixing mistakes. It isn’t. It’s about tracing a thought back to the moment it went wrong. Understanding that the bug didn’t show up by chance. It existed because something was trusted that shouldn’t have been. Bugs don’t appear. They surface.
